# Creative studio deep-run seed template

This template exists only to support the task-specific four-day studio experiment. It is not a universal workflow contract.

## Seed mission

Develop an original comedy feature with strong visual/slapstick comedy, ensemble/competitive energy, memorable characters, emotional pressure, and a feature-length story that is genuinely worth watching rather than merely structurally complete.

The studio has no artificial task-count, token, or cost ceiling for this experiment. Use deep research, competing alternatives, targeted iteration, and independent challenge where they materially improve judgment quality.

## Research horizon

Use up to four calendar days of active work. Do not manufacture idle work merely to consume time, and do not terminate because artifact fields are filled. Spend the budget on unresolved mission-critical judgments, evidence, alternatives, scene tests, criticism, and revision. If the runtime cannot remain continuously active across the whole wall-clock window, persist a resumable checkpoint rather than pretending the research window was completed.

## Organizational requirement

RUN-AUTHORITY delegates the mission to one ROOT and does not solve it. ROOT must first identify the small set of mission-critical judgments, unresolved uncertainties, and failure modes that could make a technically complete movie substantively poor. Only then may ROOT design the studio organization.

The organization must be allowed to become large and hierarchical when justified. Department-like responsibilities may receive delegation and child-acceptance authority when their domain contains materially distinct unresolved judgments. Do not default every child to leaf status. Research must be a team function with multiple independent responsibilities and synthesis, not one generic researcher.

The following judgment domains must have explicit accountable ownership somewhere in the graph, but they are not prescribed role names or a fixed hierarchy:

- contemporary comedy/slapstick research, especially 2024–2026;
- premise/originality and concept selection;
- character identity, relationships and scene behavior;
- comic voice, visual/physical comedy and set-piece design;
- story causality, pacing and escalation;
- scene design and storyboard execution;
- emotional/psychological stakes;
- independent red-team challenge against generic or merely well-formatted output;
- final editorial integration and greenlight.

## Research standard

Use web research whenever factual external scope is present. Separate publication date from event/evidence date when relevant. Prefer primary/authoritative craft sources where available, combine them with critic/audience/community evidence where useful, preserve attribution, and check material counterevidence. Popularity is not proof that something is funny. Research exists to expose patterns, disagreement, stale conventions, audience expectations and unexplored opportunities.

The research organization should specifically examine comedy/slapstick work and reception from 2024–2026, while using older craft/history only when it helps explain current practice. It should study what lands, what fails, and why at the level of character, performance, scene construction, visual timing, editing, escalation, ensemble dynamics, tone and audience reaction.

## Creative development standard

Do not expand the first plausible premise into a full outline. Preserve multiple materially different directions long enough to make real choices. An accountable owner must explain why a direction advances, what credible alternatives lost, and what unresolved weakness remains.

Characters are not accepted because they have a profession plus an ironic opposite trait or a labeled comic engine. Important characters must be tested through desire, status, relationships, pressure behavior, shame/fear, misbelief, abrasiveness/likability, specificity and actual scene interactions.

Comedy mechanisms are not accepted because they can be named. Major comic scenes and slapstick set pieces must be developed as concrete audience-visible cause-and-effect: setup, information asymmetry, physical geography, timing, escalation, reversal, character commitment, payoff and later consequence.

A storyboard is downstream evidence of developed scenes, not a substitute for them.

## Iteration standard

Accepted child work can still expose a new mission-critical uncertainty. Acceptance closes the delegated obligation; it does not prove the domain is exhausted. If a material unresolved judgment could change or replace the current premise, characters, comedic identity, set pieces, emotional stakes or feature structure, create an explicit follow-up contract with the appropriate existing responsibility or a newly justified responsibility.

Before greenlight, require at least one independent challenge cycle against the assembled concept and targeted revision of any weakness that could materially alter audience value. Do not use a checklist-presence test as the sole basis for final acceptance.

## Final deliverable

Return a coherent film package only after the organization has earned convergence. Include the final title/logline, world/premise, principal characters, complete feature story, major scenes/set pieces, storyboard/beat board, tonal/comedic rationale, emotional stakes, research synthesis, rejected directions and why they lost, integration traceability, unresolved risks, and a concise greenlight basis explaining why the studio believes this concept is genuinely stronger than the plausible alternatives it explored.
