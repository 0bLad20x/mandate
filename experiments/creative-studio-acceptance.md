# Creative studio acceptance boundary

Task-specific acceptance guidance for the four-day experiment.

The final studio result is not accepted merely because it contains all requested fields. Acceptance must include evidence that the studio made and challenged the mission-critical judgments that determine substantive creative quality.

Required evidence:

1. The organization was derived from explicit creative failure modes and unresolved judgments rather than output headings alone.
2. Research was executed by multiple independent responsibilities and synthesized with attribution and material counterevidence.
3. Multiple materially distinct premise directions were explored before convergence, with rejected directions and reasons preserved.
4. Principal characters were pressure-tested through relationships and scenes; one-line ironic oppositions were not accepted as sufficient character identity.
5. Major comic/slapstick sequences were developed concretely at audience-visible scene level, not merely named as mechanisms.
6. At least one independent challenge cycle materially tested genericity, predictability, tonal weakness, character weakness, and scene-level comic value.
7. Any material weakness discovered during review was either resolved by an explicit follow-up contract or carried upward as an unresolved risk; it was not silently reclassified as a formatting/integration detail.
8. Final greenlight records why the selected concept is stronger than credible alternatives and why the studio believes further work is unlikely to materially replace the core direction.

These are experiment acceptance conditions, not foundation governance.
